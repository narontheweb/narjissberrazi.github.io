summary: Played a leading role in shaping AI strategy, governance and organisational adoption — translating regulation, business needs and emerging AI opportunities into a target operating model, clear decision structures, prioritised use cases, safe experimentation and practical training.
context: The organisation was expanding its use of AI across business and IT while needing a consistent approach to governance, regulatory compliance, safe experimentation, prioritisation and organisational adoption.
challenge: AI opportunities were emerging across the organisation, but there was no single structure defining what could be done with AI, how tools and agents should be governed, how sensitive information should be handled, how use cases should be assessed, or how employees should be trained and supported.
contribution:
- Played a leading role in shaping the AI strategy and Target Operating Model.
- Translated EU AI Act requirements and broader responsible-AI principles into practical governance.
- Used regulatory requirements as guidance and checklist criteria when evaluating AI use cases.
- Helped establish and structure a 12-person cross-functional AI working group with management, business and IT representatives.
- Defined roles, responsibilities, councils, decision rights and escalation paths.
- Assessed 10 AI use cases and supported prioritisation across value, feasibility, risk and readiness.
- Developed a prioritisation matrix and roadmap.
- 'Defined governance around AI tools and AI agents: what could and could not be done, safe experimentation, privacy-sensitive information, company-sensitive data and technical sandbox principles.'
- Supported the setup of a safe technical sandbox / POC approach for experimentation.
- Created management decision materials and facilitated workshops.
- Designed and delivered a mandatory AI training programme.
